Our lab primarily focuses on the intersection of cancer metabolism, antigen presentation and tumor immunology. We are particularly interested in how mitochondrial function and metabolic perturbation regulate MHC-I mediated antigen presentation, thereby influencing tumor immunogenicity and responsiveness to immunotherapy. More broadly, Our work aims to define how metabolic vulnerabilities of cancer cells can be leveraged to enhance immune recognition and therapeutic efficacy.

We are currently investigating the role of metabolites, through interference of metabolite carriers, in regulating tumor immunogenicity. By systematically knocking out these carriers, we aim to define how metabolic disturbances alter antigen presentation and immune visibility. In addition, we conduct genetic (CRISPR-Cas9 library) and chemical (compound library) screens to identify novel regulators of MHC-I surface expression, a critical determinant of immune recognition.

Our goals are to elucidate the link between cellular metabolism and develop therapeutic strategies that restore or enhance tumor antigen presentation. We aim to increase the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors and pursue broader questions about tumor biology, including age-related differences in tumor development and immunotherapy response. We am also striving to pioneer new technologies for monitoring immune responses and identify synergistic drug–metabolite combinations.

Beyond these aims, We are also interested in broader fundamental questions about life and disease. For example, why do tumors arising in children differ from those in older individuals, and how do these differences influence their responses to immunotherapy? We are also interested in technological development, particularly methods to monitor immune responses in real time, and drug synergy, exploring which metabolites or chemical probes can render tumor cells highly sensitive to specific therapies.
